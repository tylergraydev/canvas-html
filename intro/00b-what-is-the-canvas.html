What is a canvas?

A <canvas> is the opposite of the DOM tree: it's a single blank rectangle of pixels with nothing inside it — no elements, no nodes, no text the browser knows about. You get a drawing surface and a "brush" (a context), and you paint every dot yourself in JavaScript. It comes in two flavours: a 2D brush for shapes and text, and a WebGL brush that talks to the GPU for real 3D. Here's one tiny example of each.

A bitmap, not a tree

Whatever you draw is just coloured dots.

There are no child nodes to inspect, no labels, nothing selectable. A screen reader sees one anonymous "canvas" and nothing more. That's the trade: total drawing freedom, zero built-in structure — the exact mirror image of the DOM.

Two brushes

getContext('2d') gives you paths, fills, gradients and text — great for the heart below. getContext('webgl2') gives you the GPU and shaders — that's how the lit, bobbing ball (and its soft shadow) is drawn entirely in a tiny fragment program.

Notice what's missing

          

            Both examples look rich, but to the page they're a single opaque rectangle. You can't
            select the heart, you can't Tab to the ball, a screen reader has nothing to announce.
            That's canvas: pixels, full stop. Now compare it with the DOM
            primer — and the next concept shows the trick that lets a canvas paint live DOM and keep
