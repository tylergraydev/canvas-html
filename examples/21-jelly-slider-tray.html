Jelly slider, tray edition (WebGL)

A second take on the gel slider — a dependency-free WebGL2 port of TypeGPU's Jelly Slider (itself inspired by Voicu Apostol). The soft body is a Verlet chain smoothed into quadratic béziers, rasterised into a signed-distance field, then ray-marched as a 3D tube — with a rounded pie cap on the handle — that rests in a rounded pill tray. Ambient occlusion sinks it into the groove, a fake shadow and caustic highlight spill onto the tray, and temporal anti-aliasing smooths every frame. The percentage is a live DOM element uploaded to the GPU with texElementImage2D() and printed on the tray.

Two jelly sliders, two recipes

The previous demo ports the WICG webgpu-jelly-slider: a chain rasterised as straight segments on a flat light tray, with the value spelled out in words. This one ports the TypeGPU demo, which is a different beast. Between the 17 chain points it fits quadratic béziers (control points solved from the per-point normals) so the gel reads as one smooth blob rather than a polyline. The ground is not a flat plane but a sdRoundedBox2d pill tray the tube sinks into, lit with ambient occlusion, a béziertexture-projected fake shadow, caustic highlights and warm bounce light from the gel. The handle end gets a real rounded cap (sdPie extruded), the jelly refracts the tray through Beer–Lambert absorption with forward scatter, and a neighbourhood-clamped TAA pass (Halton camera jitter + history blend) removes the ray-march shimmer. WebGL's texElementImage2D() — the counterpart of the WebGPU copyElementImageToTexture() the original uses — uploads the live numeric readout straight to the GPU to be printed on the tray.
