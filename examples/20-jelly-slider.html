Jelly slider (WebGL)

A dependency-free WebGL2 port of the WICG webgpu-jelly-slider. The bar is a ray-marched translucent gel: a soft-body chain (Verlet physics, computed on the CPU) is rasterised into a signed-distance field, then a fragment shader marches a 3D tube through it with Fresnel reflection, refraction, Beer–Lambert absorption and a specular highlight, resting on a light tray with a soft shadow. Drag the handle — the gel stretches and wobbles. The slider is a real range input, and the spelled-out percentage is a live DOM element uploaded to the GPU with texElementImage2D() and painted onto the tray.

How the gel gets its look

A 17-point chain runs from a left anchor to the handle (the slider's value). Verlet integration with distance, bending and arch constraints makes it buckle and wobble like a soft body. Each frame the chain is drawn into a small floating-point texture as a 2D distance field; the ray-marcher samples that field, extrudes it into a 3D tube, and shades the surface as glassy jelly: Fresnel reflection at grazing angles, a refracted ray tinted by Beer–Lambert absorption (so it deepens orange where it's thick), forward scatter, and a tight specular highlight. The tray is a lit plane with a projected soft shadow and the spelled-out percentage. WebGL exposes texElementImage2D() — the counterpart of the WebGPU copyElementImageToTexture() the original uses — to upload that live DOM text straight to the GPU.
