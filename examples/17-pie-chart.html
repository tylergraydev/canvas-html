Accessible pie chart

Ported from the WICG spec's pie-chart demo. The wedges are drawn with canvas-native radial gradients, but every label is a real, focusable DOM node painted around its slice with drawElementImage(). Press Tab to walk the slices — each one explodes out and gets a genuine canvas focus ring via drawFocusIfNeeded(). The chart stays a proper list in the accessibility tree, which is the whole point of the API.

Why draw labels as DOM?

Canvas charts have always had an accessibility problem: the wedges are just pixels, and any text you fillText() is invisible to a screen reader. Here the labels are ordinary <div role="listitem" tabindex="0"> elements, so the chart is a navigable list, the percentages are selectable text, and focus rings are real. The paint loop draws each gradient wedge, then calls drawElementImage(label, x, y) at the slice's midpoint and writes the returned matrix back to the label's transform so its hit-box and focus ring line up with the painted pixels. A short spring lerp on each slice's radial offset gives the explode-on-focus motion, driven by a requestAnimationFrame loop that runs only while a slice is moving and then idles.
